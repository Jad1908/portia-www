lede: "Every decision persists as YAML in your repo — the operation, the keys, the prediction the checks made at the time, and the sentence saying why. That spec compiles to one committed .sql per table, dbt-shaped, so what you hand over is SQL and what gets reviewed in a PR is a diff."
---

The spec holds what SQL cannot: the `rationale`, the `expect` block recording
what was predicted when the decision was made, and the `grain` claim about what
one output row is meant to be. The `.sql` is a build output — regenerated, never
hand-edited — and committed anyway, because the pipeline is the deliverable and
someone has to read it.

Run it again next quarter against a source that has shifted underneath you and
**drift is reported against those predictions**, rather than found later in a
dashboard. A downstream spec refers to an upstream table by its plain name; no
paths, no `depends_on` list. portia scans the specs and derives the build order
itself.
